# What the matrix says (first pass, 2026-10-18)

1. In every paper whose full text I could scan (Shone & Hillel, DeepAM, Kim & Bansal, Borysov, Abbasi) the keyword scan found NO use of survey weights or survey sampling error in the evaluation. Several treat the available sample as "the population" (Kim & Bansal: a >1M-person sample as the h-population; Abbasi: the Montreal OD survey "treated as a representative" dataset). This is consistent with the "framing" gap, but it is NOT a systematic proof: 5 of about 11 candidate papers were scanned, the rest by abstract only.
2. Closest neighbour for "uncertainty in evaluation": Mensah et al. 2025 (read 2026-10-19). They bootstrap the records i.i.d., refit CTGAN/VAE on each bootstrap sample and report percentile CIs of MAE/MSE/RMSE over Montreal OD surveys 2008/2013/2018 (tabular attributes). No survey weights, expansion factors, strata or replicate weights appear in the text. Their CI measures how much a model's error varies when it is retrained on resampled data, not how much a held-out real sample differs from the training sample under the survey design. So it is related but does NOT cover our design-based (JKn) noise floor, the real-vs-real C2ST null, the weighted/unweighted frame effect, or activity schedules. Cite and differentiate; do not claim "first uncertainty quantification in this field".
3. Closest neighbour for the statistic itself: Snoke et al. 2018 (pMSE with an analytic null; standardised differences). Our z and C2ST are applications of that line to survey-designed data. The novelty can only be the design-based (JKn) variance and the effect of the evaluation frame, not the test.
4. Lautrup et al. 2025: CART and Bayesian networks often beat deep generators, and metrics disagree. This supports our CART finding and weakens any claim that "metric-dependence" is new in tabular synthesis.
5. check_gap_saturation returned unusable counts. The gap remains "to verify". Required: opposite-view search, citation chasing from Mensah / Snoke / Kapp, a survey-statistics search (design-based evaluation of predictive models), full read of Kapp 2023.
